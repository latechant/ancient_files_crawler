南洋孔教会创会人之一 
陈延谦先贤简介 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
陈延谦（1881-1943.3 ） ，字逊南、益吾、同安莲花澳溪人。积极支持孙
中山的民主革命；曾任道南等多所华文学校董事长，中华总商会董事，新加
坡同安会馆发起人之一，并任首届主席。经商和从政之余，醉心于诗文写作，
著有《止园集》 。 
18 岁到新加坡谋生，1909 年与友人合办商店，后扩大为公司赢利甚多。
后与林文庆等人创办华侨银行。曾一度任新加坡同盟会会长，汇款支持蔡锷。
抗战期间捐款救灾，被选为南侨总会常务委员。在家乡创办同美车路公司，
修建同安集美公路。在同安独资捐办澳溪小学，捐款赞助英华学校等。在新
加坡投资开发东海岸，兴建商店、市场和住宅，人称为“延谦坊” 。

文化在南洋这片土地上的传承与发展。这不仅是陈延谦先生的愿望，也是陈

修身与治国——《论语》中的君子教育 
刘奕阳 
新加坡国立大学中文系博士生 
“君子”这一概念在儒家思想中非常重要。先秦典籍中，我们看到先贤
频繁使用“君子”来指涉他们心中理想的人格。 “君子”一词的最初含义，
指掌握权力，可以发号施令的贵族阶层，与个人的道德修养尚无直接紧密的
联系。譬如， 《尚书》中就有言“惟截截善谝言，俾君子易辞，我皇多有之！ ”
1 这里的“君子”一词指王臣官员，是一种职位的代称。随着时间的推移，
“君子”逐渐开始代指有德行的统治者。在《诗经》中，就频繁出现“淑人
君子” “乐只君子” “显允君子”等描绘。2 均在代表贵族身份的同时强调了
他们的内在德行。而在《论语》中，孔子认为的“君子”一词已经发展出了
多种意涵，既可以指代传统意义中的君主，为官者，贵族，又可以指纯粹的
有道德之人，而不论其政治背景。 3 总体而言， 《论语》中的“君子”一词处
在传统意涵与新定义的交汇阶段，孔子从道德标准的角度为“君子”增添了
种种行事准则，但本意仍是强调国家的管理者应当具有良好的内在德行和处
理各类问题的能力。也就是说， 《论语》当中的“君子”教育仍然看重社会
责任和政治责任的培养，而并非单纯地进行道德修养的提升。

当代学者们对于儒家文化中的“君子”概念进行剖析时，通常有两种方
式：一种是将孔子的君子理论视为一种道德人格的养成方式，即着重落在了
个人的“修身”层面，进而将《论语》中孔子对于学生们的君子教育，理解
为一种伦理生活的建构，进而指出这样一种哲学的最大魅力是解决人生的大
问题。4 另外一种解读方式则是将儒家同西方宗教进行比较，进而在这种思
考框架之下，将“君子”定位为社会阶层中的精英，并且是社会文化的代表。
他们可以充当诸侯的幕僚，也可以追求官职，谋求收入。 5 这两种解读路径
各自有其深厚的解释背景，也符合我们通常对于《论语》中“君子”概念的
印象式理解。然而，正如上文对君子一词含义的梳理，君子—看似是一种道
德人格的描述，背后实际蕴含着政治职责的意味。君子所应当具备的道德修
养，所对应的是他们肩负的国家管理责任。余英时先生也曾在著作中论述
“君子”这一概念的来由。他认为，在最初“君子”是指社会上居于高位的
人，后来才逐渐转化为道德名称。最初是少数王侯贵族的专号，后来才慢慢
变成上下人等都可用的通称。

中國中古文章中「天道輔仁」的運用 
及其思想來源考索 
张鑫诚 
新加坡国立大学中文系博士生 
摘要 
中國中古文章中，文人描述死亡情境時，常使用「輔仁」之語彙，本文
發現實乃「天道輔仁」之省略，而並非《論語》中的「以友輔仁」 。此情景
下文人用「輔仁」來指代天道，來表述書寫對象的死亡過程，由此給予死者
高尚的道德評價，或某些政治情境下上天的支持。同時筆者發現「天道輔仁」
實際上是源於《左傳》中的「神福仁而禍淫」思想。由於福、輔二字在中古
有著十分相近的語音，同時受到逐漸經典化的《論語》中「以友輔仁」的影
響。隨著「輔仁」在文章中被不斷被使用，以及文學作品的經典化，其傳統
力量不斷被強化，導致魏晉南北朝時期「天道輔仁」逐漸取代「福仁」成為
既定傳統。 
關鍵詞：輔仁、天道、中古、 《左傳》 、中國思想 
1. 前言 
中國中古文章中常出現「輔仁」之語彙，尤其在與死亡有關的文體中
（碑、誄、哀辭、祭文、墓志） ，然而其語意指涉及背後的哲學思想並未被
今人所習察。

4. 從「福仁」到「輔仁」演化 
「 （天道）輔仁」之義理儘管明顯源自《左傳》中「神福仁而禍淫」 ，然
此語彙概念並未在任何先秦文獻中出現。 「 （天道）輔仁」在魏晉南北朝時出
現，意涵與使用情境與「天福仁」幾乎完全相同，同時逐步成為被使用的主
流，一定程度上出現了從「天福仁」到「天輔仁」的演化更替。 
筆者認為出現從「福仁」到「輔仁」 ，很可能是受《論語》中「以友輔
仁」語意的廣泛流傳而導致的混用。 《論語》載： 「曾子曰：君子以文會友，
以友輔仁。」孔安國傳曰： 「友相切磋之道，所以輔成已之仁」又正義曰：
「此章以論友，言君子之人，以文德會合朋友，朋友有相切磋𤥨𤥨磨之道，所
以輔成已之仁德也。」
31  顯然此處「輔仁」是指良友輔弼仁道之踐行，與
「天道輔仁」中指上天賜福仁者的指涉有很大差異。

5. 結語 
本文發現中古時期人們面對死亡或災異時，保有一種上天庇佑仁善者的
思想。因此文人常在文章使用「天道輔仁」辭句，或有時直接用「輔仁」來
指代天道，來表述書寫對象的死亡過程，或某些政治情境下上天的支持。由
此給予死者高尚的道德評價，安慰死者和生者，同時以質疑天道的方式表達
深刻的惋惜，這是中古以來的文學新風貌。 
但若不追根索源明確「天道輔仁」之思想淵源，則會誤以為「天道輔仁」
是魏晉人的新觀念，從而對中古思想文化產生偏頗的認識。筆者發現「天道
輔仁」實際上是源於《左傳》中的「神福仁而禍淫」思想。然而隨著歷史發
展「輔仁」逐漸取代了「福仁」 ，筆者證明福、輔二字在中古有著十分相近
的語音，同時也可能受到逐漸經典化的《論語》中「以友輔仁」的影響。 
隨著「輔仁」在文章中被不斷被使用，以及文學作品的經典化，其傳統
力量不斷被強化，導致唐代之後「天道輔仁」成為既定傳統。在文化傳統中
「輔仁」雖然晚出，卻後來居上逐漸比「福仁」更常被使用，可以觀照到中
古中國人們，會用不同的語彙來表達同樣思想觀念，出現了思想的同質 -典

“内圣外王” ：浅论孙中山的天下观 
汪梦桥 
新加坡国立大学中文系硕士生 
摘要 
1911 年，在辛亥革命的浪潮中，中国最后的封建王朝走向历史的终结。
鉴于新生的共和国家仍处于列强环伺之下，因此，如何确立本国与亚洲邻国
及西方列强的关系就成为了执政者必须面对的问题。为了完成中国的国家构
建，身为革命精神领袖的孙中山（ 1866-1925） ，一方面通过宣扬汉族领导下
的“五族共和”完成国民意识的塑造，同时，在传统的民族国家的语境之外，
孙中山还试图通过中国数千年以来的儒学传统建构起一套超越近代国家“国
界”与“民族”观念的，经由“王道”与“德化”成立的“中华”系统。这
套系统不但在此后中日两国的论战与斗争中扮演了重要的角色，更是对当今
中国的对外政治产生了深远的影响。本文就孙中山所主张的“大亚洲”系统
展开讨论，分析孙中山“天下观”的儒学本源及具体表现，并最终尝试对传
统儒学在构建 20 世纪多民族统一体过程中的意义进行讨论。 
关键词：孙中山、王道、民族国家、中华、五族共和

4. 结论 
综上所述，传统儒学所主张的“内圣外王”在孙中山的天下观的形成中
起到了关键的作用。在国内的民族主义建构过程中，在孙中山的天下观与民
族建构之中，多民族统一体的建立有时并不一定需要依靠强力的政治体制或
同化的语言习俗，而更多的是依赖共同的价值导向（道德与文化）与紧密的
经济联系（朝贡体系） 。为了实现民族的平等与解放，孙中山在反清革命期
间强调“排满兴汉” 。而伴随着民国的建立，孙中山通过由传统儒家道德联
结起来的“五族”统一为中华民族。 
另一方面，为了确定中华民族与亚洲诸民族乃至世界民族间的关系，孙
中山主张以“王道德化”构建起亚洲民族的联盟，并藉由强大的政治、军事
实力与千年的道德、文化实现世界大同的梦想。这也使得我们对传统儒学所
主张的道德、文化在处理复杂国际事件中可以起到的重要作用有了更加深刻
的认识。

前言 
蕅益智旭（1599—1655 ）注解《論語》一書而成《 〈論語〉點睛》 ，以佛
理闡之釋之，藉以顯真諦，發聖賢心。而在其闡發下，儒釋兩道似乎於「聖
賢心學」處相匯相通。蕅益道人是如何做到這點的？這是本篇想要回答的問
題。學生特未翻看蕅益道人之佛宗觀點，欲以無知之心於注釋文本中察蕅益
道人的思想論點，並作整理。而後再結合對應的論語文本，觀察蕅益道人在
這個思想的角度又是如何解釋論語中的仁、忠、信、孝等具體概念。 
1. 《論語點睛》中的蕅益道人 
1.1. 說「學」 
澫益道人在「學」之一字上所繫的重要性不可謂不大， 《點睛》中也是
抓住了每一次強調的機會。在他看來， 「人人本有靈覺之性」 （ 《論語·學而》
“學而時習之”下注） ，謂「厚，是本性之德。復其本性，故似歸家」 （ 《論
語·學而》“慎重追遠”下注） ，只是因為有所迷而遭物累、不悅，故要想
恢復靈覺之本性，則需「覺」 ，而「始覺之智」即是學。可見覺有多麼重要，
學就有多麼重要，所謂「學者，覺也」 。學是覺的開始、前提，學後有覺，
覺後時時覺，方無物累，無不悅，脫此本體之迷，達本性。 

【PDF页42】
38 
 
關於學與思的關係，蕅益道人言「學而不思。即有聞無慧。思而不學。
即有慧無聞。罔者，如人數他寶，自無半錢分也。殆者，如增上慢人，墮坑
落塹也」 （ 《論語·爲政》“學而不思”下注） 。 「增上慢」佛經作解「於未證
得殊勝德中，謂已證得，名增上慢」 ，即學而不思不會有任何收穫， 「自無半
錢」 ，思而不學則最終也是無法精進， 「墮坑落塹」 。兩相比較，蕅益道人似
是覺得學比思更重要一些，但他也在《論語》 「吾嘗終日不食，終夜不寢，
以思，無益。不如學也」章下注「學，思，本非兩事。言此，以救偏思之失
耳」 ，可見未必如此覺得。 
又談真正好學，是謂「無怒無過，本覺之體。不遷不貳，始覺之功。此
方是真正好學」 （ 《論語·雍也》“孰為好學”下注） 。學而始覺，是以有始
覺之功，能夠不遷怒，不貳過，而要復無怒無過的本覺，則要不斷地學，不
斷地覺，所以說「此方是真正好學」 。由此可見覺之本身也是有境界差異，
學為始覺，不斷地學方至本覺，關於這一點，蕅益道人有論述： 「覺不被迷
情所動，謂之立。覺能破微細疑網，謂之不惑。覺能透真妄關頭，謂之知天
命。覺六根皆如來藏，謂之耳順。覺六識皆如來藏，謂之從心所欲不踰矩，
此是得心自在。若欲得法自在，須至八十九十，始可幾之」（ 《論語·爲政》
“吾十有五而志與學”下注） ，雖然覺有止境， 「得法自在」即是，但於人來
說， 「須至八十九十。始可幾之」 ，也相當是學無止境一說了。 
又談「學之本也」是乃實踐， 「自置其身於聖賢之列，此即學之本也。
事親事君交友，皆躬行實踐。克到聖賢自期待處，所以名為實學」 （ 《論語·
學而》“賢賢易色”下注） 。學本為覺始，好學精進以至靈覺本體，實踐則

能讓自己更接近聖賢，是學在現實生活中的體現，所謂「實學」即相對於學
的另一面——「覺」而言。 
1.2. 說「心」 
《〈論語〉點睛》中關於「心」的表達較之於「學」更多，尤見其對

更是明顯，觀心為溫故，觀心為知新，一切從心上得。

結合上述，我們可以通過《〈論語〉點睛》中這些瑣碎內容對蕅益道人
的基本思想聊作一窺，即人本有靈覺之性，但常為所迷，學而有覺，通過不
斷的學以讓自己達到無時不覺的程度，則可謂通血脈，好學之意即在此。同
時注重實踐，即「實學」 ，在實踐中向聖賢靠近。人本身既有靈覺之性，又
能通過學以達「覺」 ，那麼反求之於己，磨練自己的「心上功夫」 ，也是必然。 
2. 蕅益道人的《論語》 
2.1. 說「仁」 
a. 為仁，正是為人。不仁，便不可為人矣。作亂之本，由於好犯上。犯上之
本，由於不孝弟。不孝弟，由於甘心為禽獸。若不肯做衣冠禽獸，必孝弟以
為人。為人，即仁義禮智自皆具足。故孝弟，是仁義禮智之本。蓋孝弟，是
良知良能。良知良能，是萬事萬物之本源也。 （ 《論語·學而》“其爲人也孝
弟”下注） 
b. 仁，性體也。 （ 《論語·里仁》“惟仁者”下注） 
c. 仁體即是本來至極之體，猶所雲念佛心即是佛也。 （ 《論語·述而》“仁遠
乎哉”下注） 
d. 巧言，口為仁者之言也。令色，色取仁也。仁，是心上工夫。若向言色處
下手，則愈似而愈非。 （ 《論語·學而》“巧言令色”下注） 
觀上列蕅益道人對《論語》中的仁的闡發，可見其將仁與心緊密聯繫在
一起，非常有意思。仁是為本來至極之性體，即人心自具，有此仁則是仁人，
所謂念佛心即是佛，那何以有不仁人？還是因為心，因其甘心為禽獸，自輕

41 
 
自賤。如何避免這種情況？答案還是在於心，得良知良能，則自然「不肯做
衣冠禽獸」 ，良知自是發於心。所以蕅益道人論仁，處處不離心，而對心的
關注正是其思想的重要部分。將仁視為性體也與其所謂「人人皆有靈覺之性」
的說法相呼應。仁、靈覺乃人性之具足，於己心處修行，則覺其本性，脫其
迷體。其在《論語·里仁》 「子曰，苟志於仁矣，無惡也」條下注「千年暗
室，一燈能破」即有此意。 
2.2. 說「忠信」 
a．忠，則直心正念真如。信，則的確知得自己可為聖賢。正是自重之處。
（ 《論語·學而》“君子不重”下注） 
b. 求放心，莫若格物致知。孝弟謹信，乃至學文。皆格物致知之功也。直教
一切時文行合一而修，不是先行後文。蓋文，是道統所寄。孝弟忠信等，即
是文之實處。 （ 《論語·學而》“弟子入则孝”下注） 
c. 不信自己可為聖賢，如何進德修業。 （ 《論語·爲政》“人而無信”下注） 
據《佛學大辭典》記，直心指「正直而無諂曲之心也」 ，正念是「真正
之念，八正道之一。又作諦意」 ，真如則是「本身本來具足染淨善惡諸法」 ，
忠在於自身修養，信則是相信自己可為聖賢。蕅益大師有言「期心於大聖大
賢，名為自重」 （ 《論語·學而》“君子不重”下注） ，自重則親師取友，擇
仁而處，注重自我性靈，則人性本具的靈覺可得恢復，故其也有「道以宅心，
反不擇仁而處。何其重軀殼，而輕性靈也」 （ 《論語·里仁》“里仁為美”下
注）之歎。有此信則自重甚，自重甚則愈注重性靈，則修行愈發精進，無此
信則反之，是以說「如何進德修業」 。b 條實際仍將忠信繫於心之一字。忠

信乃是格物致知之功，而格物致知的目的是為「放心」 。正如前文1.2 部分所
述及，蕅益道人一直强調一切從心上得。是謂「觀心為溫故」 （ 《論語·爲政》
“溫故而知新”下注） ， 「觀心為要」 （ 《論語·里仁》“以約”下注） ， 「道以
宅心」 ， 「樂是心之聲，聞其樂而知其德」 （ 《論語·八佾》“子語魯大師”下

此，此發源與「仁」關係密切，据前 2.1 部分整理，這個結論不難得出。蕅
益謂「心不違仁，得法源本」 （ 《論語·雍也》“三月不違仁”下注） ，則心
與仁本是一體。其言「仁，性體也」 （ 《論語·里仁》“惟仁者”下注） ，則
「仁」是人心具足之性，但由於受物累，迷此本體，使其蒙塵，不見仁性。
鑒於此，蕅益多次提出「觀心」 ，觀心為溫故，可知新，如同磨鏡，正是其
所謂的「君子但求諸己」之意。不斷與此處求，則心可恢復良知良能，歸其
本來面目，即「仁」 。至此，仁不可不謂心上功夫也。而不論是觀心還是恢
復仁心，其中都包含一個「覺」字。沒有覺，一切無從談起。這也是為什麽
蕅益道人將「學」字尊到了極處。 「學，始覺之智」 （ 《論語·學而》“學而
時習之”下注） ， 「學者，覺也」 （ 《論語·爲政》“吾十有五而志於學”下
注 ），學而后覺， 「期心與大聖大賢」 ，時時觀心，直至仁體。B 條中「學問，
不過求放心」 ，所求之「放心」 ，正是在學問中，時時觀心中努力恢復心之原
貌——仁。值得注意的是在蕅益大師的闡發下，忠信不再是與自己以外的別
人有關，而完全是針對自身修行。

說「孝」 
a. 根於心而生於色。孝在心，而不獨在事也。 （ 《論語·為政》“子夏問孝”

b. 只是敬父母，故期之以聖賢，不違不怨，只是到底敬父母。 （ 《論語·里仁》

c. 以犬馬養，但養口體。能養志者，乃名為敬。 （ 《論語·爲政》“子游問孝”

蕅益道人說孝言「心」與「敬」 。言「心」即謂孝乃心上功夫，而不在

相參照。養父母志則是勸導他們「背塵合覺」 ，學而覺己本性，識己靈覺，
從而擺脫所迷，跳出輪回，此為敬，也是孝之真意。 
綜上所述，蕅益道人在注解《論語》中重要且具體的概念時，如仁、忠、
信、孝等，都將其與己心、本性相聯繫，處處借之言自我修行， 《論語》一
書中仁人、君子一說也被納入到「心上功夫」之下。其謂孔子是識其真心處，
儒釋在心之一字上因蕅益道人的闡發彼此相匯相通。但這並非偶然、刻意為
之可得，明代末期陽明心學的興起與發展實已現此趨勢，乃至離經叛道的學
者李贄（1527-1602） ，其相關論述如童心說等也非橫空出世，蕅益道人此本
《點睛》中也有引用王陽明（1472-1529） 、李贄二人言語。而關於這些非偶
然背後的原因還得於文外之處尋找，此篇不作妄談。 
结语 
本篇以蕅益道人如何以僧人角度解讀《論語》為研究問題，先對《點睛》
文本進行簡單整理，從「學」 「心」兩個方面一窺蕅益道人的基本思想，而
後分析其如何將這些思想貫穿到儒家仁、忠、信、孝等幾個具體概念的闡釋

唐君毅所言的「虛靈明覺」 ，既是道德形上學，亦是道德實踐的基礎理
論，大體來說是通過工夫顯示心中的道德本體。唐先生在《中國文化之精神
價值》 、 《文化意識與道德理性》 、 《道德自我之建立》 、 《生命存在與心靈境界》
等著作當中多面陳述了虛靈明覺的概念。这里所指的道德形上學借用了牟宗
三对康德“道德形而上学”的解讀，认为“道德本心”是宇宙萬物的本體。 
虛靈明覺之心肯定了所谓“道德本心”作为本體这一说：心駕馭在超越
層與經驗層之上，謂之昭臨。人可沉浸於經驗層中，但自見其本體即可「自
立命而盡性」 。主觀的人心在這一解釋當中包括客觀的心之本體。 「虛靈明覺」
則是人之本心存在於經驗層之上的理論根據。在此「虛靈」的意思並非指心
本質是虛無的，而是將「虛」視為一種作用，使得心能夠含攝世上萬物。本
文將首先討論虛靈明覺的宋明理學脈絡，之後將討論當代學者對虛靈明覺形
上學基礎提出的三类質疑，而我將試圖為唐先生辯護。

宋明理學的理論基礎 
「虛靈」與「明覺」這兩個概念在宋明理學中有一定的脈絡。虛無的源
頭是佛家中萬物皆空的概念，能悟出「虛」之道理才是世道真理，以此連結
「虛」與道德上的真理。 「明覺」二字則關聯王陽明所說的「良知」 。 
唐君毅形上學對虛靈明覺的解釋，是由王陽明的「自然明覺」 、及其弟
子浙中學派王龍溪的「四無」脈絡下發展的。 「虛靈」或「虛」這一說法自
宋明理學與佛家宇宙觀接觸後才出現。朱熹在《大學章句集注》就曾言：
「明德者，人之所得乎天，而虛靈不昧，以具眾理而應萬事者也。」這說明
瞭從自然顯現的「明德」 ，需要心在「虛靈」的狀態攝取。 
在此可見，心在「虛靈」的狀態中即可與自然之道理連動，此連動不僅
是心單方的攝取，心在虛靈攝取的同時也將道理向外部擴散，達成光照的效
果；也就是攝取道理的同時亦通過行動進一步的實踐、擴張此道理。 「明覺」
的作用則是照明在自然中可見的道理。兩者一起，則是心體作為性理的流行
之方式。在此脈絡下，王陽明思想核心當中的「良知通過『無體』的心而得
以實踐」這一說法，可被解釋為心理的活動照明萬物，也就是人心在攝取萬
物時就自然能呈現出心靈的本體。心之本體如同光源，照明萬物的時候就顯
現出它的本體。王陽明的良知「恆照」的意義，就如同唐先生所說的心體的
「昭臨」 。王陽明說： 「蓋良知只是一箇天理自然明覺發見處，只是一箇真誠
惻怛，便是他本體。
1 」在此「明覺」描述的是照亮自然之道理的道德本心，
而心的這一能力又被王陽明稱為「良知」 。

Educational Insights of the Analects: 
Implications for the Role of Teachers 
彭妤琪 
南洋理工大学哲学系博士生 
 
Abstract: The Analects documents numerous educational thoughts of 
Confucius and his teaching activities with students. In the study of the educational 
concepts in the Analects , there is a common practice of differentiating between 
teaching and learning, as well as defining distinct entities responsible for each. 
However, both teachers and students can be the subjects of teaching and learning. 
In the process of educating students, teachers should continually learn and improve 
themselves, drawing extensive knowledge from their students. Students, in turn, 
can also teach their teachers. Teachers’ reflections on their roles can lead to mutual 
enhancement between teacher and student, fostering a reciprocal educational 
relationship to enhance overall knowledge and skills, strengthen morals, and build 
a community. 
Keywords: Reciprocal Learning; the Analects; Role of Teachers 
1. Introduction 
Confucius was a visionary educational theorist with extensive practical 
experience. His educational thoughts have been organized, summarized, and 
disseminated by many intellectuals. The Analects  chronicles Confucius’s 
educational ideas and his instructional activities with students. In studying the 
educational thoughts in the Analects, it is common to distinguish between teaching 
and learning, identifying separate entities for each: educators or teachers for 
teaching and students for learning. However, in r eality, both teachers and students 

【PDF页66】
62 
 
can be the subjects of teaching and learning. Lin’s examination of textual evidence 
regarding Confucius’ aspirations and preferences demonstrates that Confucius 
advocated learning for oneself (Lin, 2015). Li and Bo even argue that the original 
meaning of “ reciprocal learning” in the Book of Rites  is not about mutual 
development between teacher and student, but rather the teacher’s own 
development in the process of teaching and learning (Li & Bo, 2012). 
I agree that teachers should learn for themselves, and I also align with Li’s 
view that learning is a collective journey with others; it is not a one -way teacher-
student interaction, as everyone should learn from others (Li, 2017). In the process 
of educating students, teachers can learn much from their students, and students can 
also teach their teachers. In the Analects , Confucius strongly endorsed learning 
from those with good moral character or advanced knowledge, as he stated, “When 
three men meet together, one of them who is anxious to learn can always learn 
something from the other two” (Confucius, 2016: 86). In this paper, I aim to explore 
the integration of teaching and learning in the Analects  and the role teachers play 
in this blend. On one hand, the teacher as a learner is often overlooked. On the other 
hand, by describing what teachers can learn, it also showcases what students can 
teach their teachers. 
2. Views on Teaching and Learning in the Analects 
The content of learning in the Analects is not only what teachers need to impart 
to students but also what teachers themselves must learn to set a good example. 
This implies that teachers must first learn and practice these contents themselves. 
Teachers should learn for the sake of effective te aching or to impart good morals. 
From the Analects, we understand that what teachers and students should learn is 
not limited to skills or knowledge from textbooks. The content includes three 
aspects: knowledge in various fields, attitudes towards learning, and methods of 
learning.

63 
 
2.1. Content of Learning and Teaching 
Regarding the content of learning, the Analects  explicitly states: “Confucius 
through his life and teaching taught only four things: a knowledge of literature and 
the arts, conduct, conscientiousness, and truthfulness.” (Confucius, 2016: 87). 
Based on this, we can infer that the content of learning mai nly includes the 
following aspects: 
Firstly, the study of literature and the arts. Confucius placed great emphasis on 
the learning of literary knowledge. This is evident from the following quote: 
“My young friends, why do you not study poetry? Poetry calls out the 
sentiment. It stimulates observation. It enlarges the sympathies and moderates 
the resentment felt against injustice. Poetry, in fact, while it has lessons for the 
duties of social life, at the same time makes us acquainted with the animate 
and inanimate objects in nature.” (Confucius, 2016: 224) 
Poetry evokes emotions, stimulates observation, expands empathy, and 
reduces anger at injustice. In fact, poetry includes lessons on social responsibilities 
and acquaints us with both animate and inanimate nature. Although this passage 
mainly addresses poe try education, from a literary knowledge perspective, 
Confucius mainly taught his students based on the Six Classics  (Book of Poetry, 
Book of History, Book of Change, Book of Rites, Book of Music, and the Spring and 
Autumn Annals). 
Secondly, behavioral norms. Confucius said: “neglect of goodness; study 
without understanding; failure to act up to what I believe to be right; and inability 
to change bad habits: these are things which cause me constant solicitude” 
(Confucius, 2016: 78). He was concerned not only about students learning without 
thinking but also about their inability to integrate knowledge with behavior, not 
knowing how to be good people. Behavioral education is closely related to ritual 

【PDF页68】
64 
 
education, a major part of Confucian education. Without learning rituals, one cannot 
cultivate good manners. 
Thirdly, loyalty and reverence. Zengzi, in conversation with others, indicated 
the master’s life and teaching are comprised of two words: conscientiousness and 
charity (Confucius, 2016: 42). Confucius also frequently mentioned loyalty. When 
asked what benevolence is, he even replied, “In dealing with yourself, be serious; 
in business, be earnest; in intercourse with other men, be conscientiousness” 
(Confucius, 2016: 166-167). Some scholars consider this aspect of education to be 
an education of speech (Lu, 2009). Because “in what he says, his object is to be 
sincere” (Confucius, 2016: 216). Confucius highly valued the education of speech, 
accused those who with “plausible speech”, and advocated for those who were 
“slow in speech and diligent in conduct” (Confucius, 2016: 227). 
Fourthly, honesty and trustworthiness. Confucius said: “make 
conscientiousness and sincerity your first principles”, and “one should be 
circumspect and trustful” (Confucius, 2016: 211). These emphasize the importance 
of loyalty and honesty. Zengzi, as a disciple of Confucius, also mentioned: 
“A man who can love worthiness in man as he loves beauty in woman; 
who in his duties to his parents is ready to do his utmost, and in the service of 
his prince is ready to give up his life; who in intercourse with friends is found 
trustworthy in what he says, -- such a man, although men may say of him that 
he is an uneducated man, I must consider him to be really an educated man.” 
(Confucius, 2016: 6-7) 
This not only integrates the content above of learning but also shows that in 
Confucian thought, being considered well-educated or a role model does not solely 
depend on one’s educational experiences at school, but rather on the behavior 
displayed in inter actions with friends, parents, and rulers. Lu believes that the 
honesty mentioned here refers to the principle of political behavior, not the honesty 
mentioned in interactions with friends (Lu, 2009). Political education indeed forms 

【PDF页69】
65 
 
an important part of Confucian education, and many parts of the Analects  relate to 
this aspect, though this paper will not focus on this area. 
2.2. Attitude towards Learning 
Education, especially Confucian moral education, is not a quick fix. The aim 
of Confucian education is not an external and forced transformation, but an essential 
change born from an individual’s internal self-awareness. Therefore, having a good 
attitude towards learning and acquiring the ability to learn is crucial. 
Students must possess a firm intention to take responsibility for their own 
learning. Confucius said, 
“In my method of teaching, I always wait for my student to make an effort 
himself to find his way through a difficulty, before I show him the way myself. 
I also make him find his own illustrations before I give him one of my own. 
When I have pointed out the bearing of a subject in one direction and found 
that my student cannot himself see its bearings into other directions, I do not 
then repeat my lesson.” (Confucius, 2016: 80) 
Confucius’ educational philosophy appears to caution teachers against the 
impulse to indoctrination, but in reality, it encourages students to fully engage in 
self-exploration under the guidance of their teachers. If students lack enthusiasm 
and willingness to learn, the efforts of teachers to help them will be in vain. Students 
not only become unappreciative but also tend to seek shortcuts, ultimately leading 
to the loss of independent thinking. 
Another important principle of learning attitude is recognizing that we are born 
to learn, not born with knowledge. This aligns with UNESCO’s four pillars of 
education proposed in 1986: learning to know, learning to do, learning to live 
together, and most importantly, learning to be. Acknowledging one’s ignorance and 
then striving to learn is a form of wisdom. “I am not one born with understanding. 
I am only one who has given himself to the study of Antiquity and is diligent in 

【PDF页70】
66 
 
seeking for understanding in such studies” (Confucius, 2016: 86), Confucius highly 
endorsed this quality of eagerness to learn. He even stated, “Even in a very small 
town there must be men who are as conscientious and honest as myself, only they 
have not tried to cultivate themselves as I have done” (Confucius, 2016: 62). This 
not only demonstrates Confucius’ passion as a teacher but also shows that he always 
considered himself a student, someone forever willing to learn, maintaining an 
ardent attitude towards self-cultivation throughout his life. 
Conversely, some are born with mediocre talents and are unwilling to exert 
themselves for understanding. Confucius said, 
“There are, perhaps, men who propound theories which they themselves 
do not understand. That is a thing I never do. I read and learn everything and, 
choosing what is excellent, I adopt it; I see everything and take note of what I 
see: that is, perhaps, next to having a great understanding.” (Confucius, 2016: 
88) 
People read widely, learned various behaviors, selected the good parts to 
absorb, observed countless things, and made records: this perhaps means having a 
rather profound understanding. Confucius was extremely passionate about learning, 
to the point where he was so engrossed that he was oblivious to the passage of time, 
even neglecting sleep and food. He once gave the standard for “loving to learn”: 
“A wise and good man, in matters of food, should never seek to indulge 
his appetite; in lodging, he should not be too solicitous of comfort. He should 
be diligent in business and careful in speech. He should seek the company of 
men of virtue and learning, in order to profit by their lessons and example. In 
this way, he may become a man of real culture.” (Confucius, 2016. 11) 
When Duke Ai of Lu asked Confucius which disciple was truly eager to learn, 
Confucius replied, “There was Yan Hui. He never made others suffer for his own 
annoyances. He never did a wrong thing twice. But unfortunately, he died in the 
prime of his life. No w there in no one, none who can be said to be a man of real 

【PDF页71】
67 
 
culture.” (Confucius, 2016: 64). He especially emphasized the absence of anger and 
arbitrary blame, reflecting Confucius’ focus on cultivating students’ moral 
sensibilities in education. 
A true lover of learning maintains a hunger for knowledge and an open-minded 
attitude, as Confucius suggested, always learning as if they have not reached their 
goal, as though they were worried about losing it. This is why students can teach 
their teachers. There are areas of brilliance in students that humble teachers are 
willing to learn from. Those who are highly sensitive, willing to learn, and not 
ashamed to seek information from those less knowledgeable than themselves can 
be regarded as well-educated. Confucius also emphasized that learners should find 
pleasure in their work and never tire of it. He said, “Those who know it are not as 
those who love it; those who love it are not as those find their joy in it” (Confucius, 
2016: 72). By maintaining a high level of interest in learning, we are less easily 
influenced by utilitarian purposes, less prone to giving up, and more willing to learn 
from others. If students enjoy learning, they naturally break free from the 
constraints of unfavorable educational environments, such as limited learning 
resources and atmospheres, strive to create learning opportunities, and delight in 
every gain. Learners who maintain joy in their studies do not feel tired but are 
motivated by their sense of identity, achievement, and belonging. 
2.3. Learning to Learn 
Confucian education emphasizes the need for learners to autonomously and 
spontaneously utilize all educational resources for meaningful learning. This 
requires students to learn how to learn, how to think, and how to acquire learning 
abilities. 
Learning ability is a comprehensive description of a learner’s learning 
motivation, attitude, methods, efficiency, innovative thinking, and creativity, aimed 
at achieving self -transformation. In the 21
st century, with rapid advancements in 

【PDF页72】
68 
 
science and technology, a student’s lifelong learning ability, which is vitality, 
creativity, and competitiveness, becomes crucial for survival. In the era of 
Confucius, he stated, “when we meet with men of worth, we should think how we 
may equal them. When we meet with worthless men, we should turn into ourselves 
and find out if we do not resemble them” (Confucius, 2016: 43), reflecting a 
consciousness of self-examination and learning habits. Internalizing learning as a 
lifelong habit for learners, elevating it to their own mission, and ultimately helping 
learners “become themselves” and “be responsible” should be prioritized over the 
acquisition of professional knowledge (Sigurðsson 2017). 
“Learning to learn” means that learners are capable of adjusting their learning 
objectives and methods. In other words, they strive to develop the ability to use 
what they have learned without overly depending on teachers. A person who is not 
adept at learning and is accustomed to “waiting, depending, and demanding” gives 
up the opportunity for growth. In Analects , “study without thinking is labour lost, 
thinking without study is perilous” (Confucius, 2016: 19) and “if a man will 
constantly go over what he has acquired and keep continually adding to it s new 
acquirements, he may become a teacher of men” (Confucius, 2016: 18) are worthy 
of emulation. One of the most valuable and scientific types of knowledge is the 
knowledge of learning methods, which affects a person’s success in learning and 
determines w hether they can become a mentor to others. The independence, 
creativity, and autonomy promoted in student learning must be built upon the 
foundation, progression, development, and integration of the curriculum. Therefore, 
teachers must effectively organize instruction, guiding students to learn for 
application, and understanding the methods of autonomous learning in a natural 
learning process. 
However, the problem is that most students are not prepared for self -directed 
education. In this context, it is inappropriate for teachers to just provide methods 
and immediate advice to students. Even many teachers cannot accept this concept, 

【PDF页73】
69 
 
still clinging to their identity as more knowledgeable transmitters than their students. 
Teachers can try to redistribute classroom power, redefine the function of teaching 
content, change the teacher’s role, adjust student behavior by emphasizing student’s 
responsibility for learning, change the process and purpose of evaluation, etc. 
Students can also enhance their feedback to teachers on aspects such as “when to 
teach, how to teach, and how to evaluate.” 
3. The Role of Teachers 
In the various factors that support students in shaping their learning content, 
attitudes, and abilities, the guiding and instructing role of teachers is crucial. If 
teachers are unwilling or unable to learn, students naturally follow the same 
learning pat terns. When teachers fail to set an example, students are likely to 
struggle, perceiving the teacher as unserious or incompetent. 
There are different perspectives on the role of teachers. In Aristotle’s view, 
teachers merely act as midwives to the students’ critical and independent thinking. 
They can only be motivators, collaborators, participants, and facilitators of students’ 
self-creation. The teacher’s responsibility is no longer merely indoctrinating 
knowledge but increasingly to stimulate thought. Beyond formal duties, a teacher 
is more like a counselor, a participant in dialogue, someone who helps in exploring 
contradictory vie wpoints rather than presenting them as truths. According to 
German educator Hans Lenk, to collaborate with the future world, teachers should 
become “the undertaker of future responsibility”. Teachers should possess 
educational ideals, hence, in their teaching, they are not only conveying knowledge, 
experience, and cognitive skills but also presenting worldviews and ethical 
perspectives. 
Confucius often employed the method of dialogic teaching, a type of 
educational activity where the roles of teachers and students shift (Woo 2019). 
Without dialogue, there is no communication, and without communication, there is 

【PDF页74】
70 
 
no real education (Freire, 2005). It requires dialoguers to be equal, open, creative, 
and to possess diverse values. When Confucius’s disciples asked his opinion on an 
issue, he would give his answer. As disciples further inquired, Confucius continued 
to r espond. Sometimes, disciples gained unexpected understandings, which 
Confucius would accept or critique, assessing their progress. For example, after Zi 
Gong discussed the Book of Poetry with Confucius, Confucius realized the progress 
of Zi Gong and said “now I can begin to speak of poetry to you. I see you understand 
how to apply the moral” (Confucius 2016: 12). 
In the process of dialogue, teachers and students are equal participants. 
Teachers sometimes act as educators, sometimes as knowledge learners being 
taught, just like students; likewise, students can also become teachers; they are 
jointly responsible for t he learning process. The teacher’s authority is no longer a 
force of coercion and domination, but a source of motivation for student learning. 
Students are no longer mere replicators of knowledge but discoverers and creators 
of knowledge. Therefore, the roles and statuses of teachers and students will be 
redefined and reshaped. 
In Confucian philosophy, teachers possess a unique role: unlike in western 
contexts, due to China’s traditional hierarchical structure, teachers assume a role 
akin to parental responsibility. On one hand, Confucius deeply cared for his 
disciples, treating them with the same affection as his own children, both in their 
schooling and personal lives. His profound sorrow upon hearing of the death of his 
favorite disciple, Yan Hui, illustrates this attachment. On the other hand, Confucius 
placed great emphasis o n family ethics, advocating that students should care for 
their families and country while pursuing knowledge and self -improvement, as 
evidenced in the following quote: 
“A young man, when at home, should be a good son; when out in the 
world, a good citizen. He should be circumspect and truthful. He should be in 
sympathy with all men, but intimate with men of moral character. If he has 

【PDF页75】
71 
 
time and opportunity to spare, after the performance of those duties, he should 
then employ them in literary pursuits.” (Confucius, 2016: 6) 
Unlike parental concern, a teacher’s care for their charges is based on the hope 
that students will lead society towards harmony. This concern extends beyond the 
immediate students in their presence. Such emotional identification links the 
present, future, and past, as well as all people. Teachers’ care for students, driven 
by the desire to guide society towards greater harmony, elevates to a responsibility 
for societal development and the human future (Sigurðsson 2017). This love and 
responsibility drive t eachers to willingly embrace this duty and strive for 
educational goals. Thus, in the educational process, teachers constantly reflect on 
their speech, teaching content, and subjects: Where do these students come from? 
Where am I leading them? Does it conv ey the right educational objectives and 
values? What impact have I had on them? What will their future be like? Some 
might ask if the unknowable future also falls within one’s responsibility. A teacher’s 
continual reflection mirrors their recognition of their professional role. Confucius 
exemplified this, frequently reflecting and questioning himself, and his disciples 
were deeply influenced by this aspect. For instance, Zengzi said, 
“I daily examine into my personal conduct on three points: first, whether 
in carrying out the duties entrusted to me by others, I have no failed in 
conscientiousness; secondly, whether in intercourse with friends, I have no 
failed in sincerity and trustwor thiness; thirdly, whether I have not failed to 
practice what I profess in my teaching.” (Confucius, 2016: 5) 
From the above discussion, it is evident that teachers, besides playing various 
roles in educational activities, should also recognize the role of a learner. Only in 
this way can they integrate the education of others with self -education to achieve 
true knowledge and understanding. For example, Confucius pursued the value of 
supreme goodness. He studied the concept of supreme goodness, found ways to 
achieve it, and applied goodness in education. Viewing educational actions as good 

【PDF页76】
72 
 
deeds is a necessary condition for teachers, as education fundamentally exists in the 
interaction between people; both teachers and students should start from the point 
of goodness, jointly pursuing it. Teachers demonstrate professional ethics through 
teaching activities, presenting good deeds and behaviors to students, providing 
them with learning models, enabling students to learn or imitate, develop good 
behaviors, and achieve educational ideals. In this process, both teachers and 
students are continuously improving themselves. 
4. Insights from the Analects on Enhancing Teacher Competencies 
To become exemplary educators and learners, teachers must possess certain 
capabilities, which they should actively seek to develop. The process of education 
is also a process of learning. In preparing lessons, teachers need to maintain 
continuous learning and updating of knowledge. Personalized teac hing, in reality, 
is a comprehensive evaluation and examination of a teacher’s judgment, knowledge, 
and practical wisdom. Through educational activities, teachers demonstrate 
professional ethics, aligning moral practice with everyday teaching. 
In terms of educational skills, teachers should understand how to teach 
according to students’ characteristics. Sometimes, even if disciples ask the same 
question, Confucius might give different answers based on the actual situation of 
the student. For ins tance, when Ziyou and Zixia asked about filial piety, Zhu Xi, 
citing Cheng Yi, noted that Ziyou could nourish his parents but lose respect, Zixia 
could be straightforward but not gentle; these are their respective deficiencies. 
Confucius tailored his answers to the student’s capabilities. Only by understanding 
the laws of student development, exploring the characteristics and behaviors of 
students at different times, and adopting timely educational forms and content can 
outstanding students be cultivated. 
Regarding emotional connections, during the teaching process, students view 
teachers as figures to turn to for guidance and assistance. This appeal encompasses 

【PDF页77】
73 
 
support in academic, psychological, and life aspects. Students may seek 
understanding, assistance, and care from teachers in the educational relationship. 
For an ideal teacher, the primary and fundamental condition is to have the attitude 
and ability to es tablish educational relationships with students (Woo 2019), 
including trust, hope, acceptance, patience, and avoiding indifference, hatred, 
cruelty, authoritarianism, and closed- mindedness. Without such an educational 
atmosphere, nurturing student personalities or realizing educational ideals becomes 
impossible. Despite Confucius’s strict hierarchical views internally, he advocated 
for no class distinction in education. He did not discriminate against any student, 
including those from impoverished backgrounds, treating them equally. Confucius 
believed in respecting the youth, as their future might not be inferior to the older 
generation, reflecting his generosity and respect for students (Conroy 1990). It was 
for these reasons that Confucius and his disciple s established admirable 
relationships revered by later generations. 
In terms of comprehensive knowledge, Confucian education emphasizes 
humanistic cultivation, necessitating a broad spectrum of knowledge from literature, 
ritual, experience, and morality (Conroy 1990). In the Greek era, Socrates 
advocated for the unity of knowledge and morality and that knowledge is a virtue. 
Therefore, both knowledge and morality were objects of pursuit for people. Besides 
professional knowledge, educators must possess high moral standards and the 
capacity for constant reflection, enabling them to view things from multiple 
perspectives and achieve the ideal of goodness. 
To clarify roles and enhance abilities, educators can consider actions 
thoroughly before taking them, understand each situation, maintain keen perception, 
and constantly reflect on emotional responses to make sound decisions. This is what 
teachers should learn from the Analects, benefiting both students and themselves.

刘华庆

Abstract: The concept of “zhi (直)” occupies an important place in the 
Analects. A brief semantic analysis of the character “ zhi” shows that it has 
multifaceted meanings such as straightness, frankness, integrity, justice or honesty 
in the Analects. A detailed textual analysis of this concept would also show that it 
is based on “ sincerity” to both self and others, governed by the principle of 
“propriety (li 礼)”, perfected by the path of “learning (xue 学)” and abiding by the 
ultimate realm of “humanity (ren 仁)”. A further investigation of this concept will 
show that it could be closely linked with the later Confucian concept of “ sincerity 
(cheng 诚)” in the Great Learning and the Doctrine of the Mean, with both 
similarities and differences. 
Keywords: Zhi, Sincerity, Propriety, Learning, Humanity

This paper will attempt to do a preliminary exploration of the concept of “ zhi 
直” in the Analects. Although this term is only mentioned in 16 out of the total 503 
conversations in the Analects which cannot be compared with those cardinal 
concepts such as “humanity (ren 仁)”, “propriety (li 礼)” or “learning (xue 学)”, it 
is still considered as an important concept in the Analects. One reason that attracts

6.2. Governed by the principle of “propriety”

conventions, from the sacrifices to ancestors down to the detail of social etiquette. 
Li in social intercourse corresponds to a considerable extent with Western

grace within the framework of fixed conve ntion, informing every action with

7. A Brief Comparison between “Zhi” in the Analects and “Sincerity”  
in the Later Confucian Texts 
According to previous analysis, the concept of “ zhi” is based on the personal 
cultivation of “ sincerity” to both self and others. This could naturally lead our 
interest towards another related question: What will be the relationship between the 
concept of “zhi” in the Analects and the concept of “ sincerity (cheng 诚)” in the 
later Confucian texts? Considering the scope and depth of this topic, an in- depth 
discussion would not be feasible in the last part of my paper, however, I would limit 
the elaboration only within the Great Learning and the Doctrine of the Mean and 
attempt to do a brief exploration of the concept of “ sincerity (cheng 诚)” in these 
two classics as well as its similarities and differences with the concept of “zhi” in 
the Analects. 
Addressed from the perspective of semantics, the meaning of the character 
“sincerity (cheng 诚)” basically refers to “ being sincere, truthful, honest without 
deceiving”, with a strong sense of moral cultivation. According to the Han-dynasty

Confucian texts.

relatively less attention than other cardinal concepts such as “propriety”, “learning”, 
“humanity”, it is closely correlated with them. After a textual analysis of the 
concerned chapters in the Analects , we could generally conclude that the concept 
of “zhi” is based on the cultivation of “ sincerity” to both self and others, without 
any forms  of deceiving; Meanwhile, “ zhi” is governed by the principle of  
“propriety (li 礼)”, with the essence of appropriateness and righteousness behind a 
set of norms, manners and etiquette; It is also perfected by the path of “ learning 
(xue 学)”  and abiding by the ultimate realm of “ humanity (ren 仁)”. In addition, 
the concept of “ zhi” is closely linked with the concept of “ sincerity (cheng 诚)” 
which generally has considerable philosophical depth in the later Confucian texts 
such as the Great Learning and the Doctrine of the Mean. Although the concepts of 
“zhi” and “sincerity” share some common meanings such as “ truthful and no 
deceiving”, the concept of “sincerity” in the Great Learning has placed a main focus 
on “no self-deception” and the concept of “sincerity” in the Doctrine of the Mean 
has uniquely embodied metaphysical and cosmological implications in describing 
the Way of Heaven while the concept of “zhi” in the Analects generally does not 
have any metaphysical implications. 
All the work presented above is by no means perfect, and it will be considered 
as purely a preliminary exploration of the topic, which would be subject to further 
revising and improving.
